Wacky Wheels builds cars just in time — no parts inventory. A late shipment, a plant outage, or a bad lot ripples into schedules, dealers, loaner cars, and owners. Messages arrive as messy emails and chats. Our Control Tower turns each one into exactly the actions the handbook demands — and proves why.

One small local language model, Qwen two point five three B, only classifies and copies phrases. Everything else is deterministic code: a knowledge graph of seventeen thousand nodes, specialist agents, a verifier, and a digital twin that dry-runs every plan before the real server sees it. The rule is simple: the LLM reads, code decides.
